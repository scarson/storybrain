# Blind Verdicts — clean-opus-hybrid2

Blind judge of generated sci-fi worlds. Scores 0-10 per axis: COHERENCE, CAUSAL/STRUCTURAL DEPTH, THEMATIC RICHNESS, NOVELTY/SURPRISE, DRAMATIC POTENTIAL.

---

## Theme 1 — uplift-nursery-world

Both worlds share the premise: a colony on an engineered planet, tended by unseen "Gardeners," who once grew a prior lineage (First Cultivars) and may be growing the humans toward a harvest. Mystery is a 5-step `reveals` chain.

### World A
- Mystery chain: `method-1-the-seeding → method-2-the-precedent → method-3-the-attention → method-4-the-shaping → method-5-the-harvest-ahead`. Clean monotonic escalation: it was made → there was a prior crop → the tools are tests → we're being edited NOW → it points forward. The chain is genuinely a thesis-building argument, each step recontextualizing the last.
- The "STEP N of the Gardeners' method" prefix in the fragment text (`method-1`..`method-5`) is **template/scaffolding leakage** — the numbering bleeds into the prose itself.
- Strong supporting structure: `powers/sense-the-aimedness` is a real mechanic ("convenience as a tell") wired to characters (Maren, Osei) and a place (`too-green-valley` strongest_at). Belief pair `the-patient-hand` vs `we-are-the-crop` maps cleanly onto faction split (Tenders=gratitude / Yield Bloc=exploitation).
- Notes are doing work, not filler: `note-key-fits-us` ("a lock that was not there at landfall"), `note-cultivars-gone` ("no graves, no war-scars — taken in"), `note-loom-edits-host` (Osei's children's blood-work) are concrete, illuminating, and each escalates dread.
- Distinctive present-tense framing: the climax (`harvest-ahead`) is explicitly AHEAD, unwitnessed — "not a fall, a culmination." Refuses the easy ruin-archaeology default.
- Minor: a few cross-drops (shaping-tool drops method-2, gene-loom drops method-1) slightly muddy the strict per-artifact attribution, but they reinforce rather than contradict.

### World B
- Mystery chain: `aimed-not-wild → tenders-keep-it → lineage-grown-before → harvested-not-killed → we-are-the-next-row`. Same escalation logic, equally coherent, and the fragment text is **clean of step-numbering** — no "STEP N" scaffolding in the prose.
- Adds a second structural spine A lacks: an `enables` chain across the six artifacts (`seed → shrine → key → loom → shaping-tool → bell`) running parallel to the `reveals` chain. This is a real gameplay/progression gate and gives the artifacts causal order, not just thematic order.
- The arc is sharper as a *decision*: `arc/should-we-ring-the-bell` with `live_risk: sounding it deliberately calls a harvest`. The bell is a usable lever the players control — far more dramatic than A's diffuse "the harvest is ahead."
- Characters are individuated by *drive* and wired into the arc with distinct verbs: Cole `wants`, Noor `resists`, Imani `must_decide`, Tomas `threatened_by`. Belief pair (grateful-guest vs fallow) is `held_by` named figures and explicitly `opposes`. This is tighter dramaturgy than A's faction-level beliefs.
- The "harvested = lifted off whole, taken UP not cut down" reveal (`frag-they-were-harvested-not-killed`, `first-cultivars.fate: ascended`) is a stronger, stranger image than A's "taken in."
- `figures/midwife-tomas-reyes sought_by the-loam-tenders` + `the-loam-tender-near-hand once let itself be half-seen` is a lovely seed of contact the Gardeners-proper never offer.
- Minor weakness: B has fewer total nodes/atmosphere notes than A; locations carry numeric `danger` fields that read slightly mechanical. The empty-cultivar-terraces are good but B leans on fewer concrete sensory hooks than A's shrine-rows/loom-hollow.

### A vs B

| Axis | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | 9 — clean chain, mechanic wired through (`sense-the-aimedness`) | 9 — dual `reveals`+`enables` spine, fully wired arc |
| Causal/Structural depth | 7 — single chain; cross-drops slightly blur attribution | 9 — parallel artifact `enables` progression + arc decision-tree |
| Thematic richness | 9 — `we-are-the-crop`, notes escalate dread concretely | 8 — strong but thinner atmospheric texture |
| Novelty/Surprise | 8 — forward-facing climax, "lock not there at landfall" | 8 — "taken UP not cut down," half-seen Tender, ring-the-bell lever |
| Dramatic potential | 7 — dread is ambient, no clear player lever | 9 — `should-we-ring-the-bell` is a real, ownable choice |
| **Total** | **40** | **43** |

**WINNER: World B.** B matches A's coherence and theme while adding a second causal spine (the artifact `enables` chain) and a genuine player-facing dramatic lever (`should-we-ring-the-bell`). A is excellent and more atmospherically dense, but its fragment prose carries literal "STEP N" template scaffolding and its climax has no actionable hinge.

**Filler/scaffolding flag:** World A leaks numbered-template scaffolding into fragment *prose* ("STEP 1 of the Gardeners' method…" through "STEP 5"). World B's fragments read as in-world claims with no numbering artifact.

## Theme 2 — extinction-plague-vector

Both worlds share an almost identical skeleton: a colony (Threshold) lands inside the range of a sealed alien quarantine; the makers (Cradlekeepers) sealed themselves into sleep beside infected sleepers (Stillborn Host) and a memetic-biological plague (the Vector). The mystery is a 5-fragment `prerequisite` chain (proof → identify → trigger → keystone → assemble) that builds toward finishing the makers' abandoned seal. Same character names (Mara, Devrant). This is a near-twin pair, so the judging is about *execution density* and *which structural choices pay off*.

### World A
- Mystery is a `prerequisite` chain with the relation edges pointing *backwards* (`identify prerequisite proof`, etc.) — i.e. proof is the prerequisite of identify, which is correct dependency semantics. Clean.
- The `arc/the-containment-race` is exceptional: explicit `win_condition`, `fail_state` ("extinction arrives by the player's own hand"), and a `clock` ("the carrier wakes on its own schedule whether or not the seal is ready"). This is the single best-articulated playable arc across both worlds in this theme.
- **Present-tense, dated event spine**: planetfall(day0) → first-relic-cracked-blind(40) → translator's-fever(50) → carrier-stirs(80) → ashline-vote(90) → keystone-located(110). The days are *load-bearing* — they sequence a rising clock and the schism lands mid-race. Devrant's blind crack (day40) `stirs` the vector, and the translator's fever (day50) is its consequence via `curse/the-reading-spreads-it`. Real causal wiring, not decoration.
- Richer cast and faction conflict: `the-ashline-creed` (burn-it-all splinter), Sergeant Vo (joins it at day90), Dr Amein (rescue), Mara (order), plus `child-tam` as the human stakes. Three competing beliefs (`makers-can-be-woken` / `every-seal-is-a-lie` / `order-is-the-only-mercy`) wired to holders.
- `curse/the-reading-spreads-it` node makes the "memetic vector travels through lore" premise mechanically concrete and ties to the translator's exposure — the standout thematic hook.
- Weakness: the cross-drop matrix is heavy — nearly every artifact `drops` 3 fragments including out-of-sequence ones (e.g. `proof-relic drops find-the-dormancy-trigger`, `sealed-vault-key drops proof`), which dilutes the clean per-artifact identity. Slightly more sprawl than necessary; a couple of locations (`orbital-quarantine-ring`, `the-cold-archive`) are thin connective tissue.

### World B
- Same `prerequisite` chain, identical fragment ordering, with the additional virtue of a **dated maker-history spine**: first-breach(100) → naming-of-the-strain(200) → dormancy-discovery(300) → keystone-cast(500) → quarantine-sleep(800, `is_pivot`). Each event `caused_by` the prior — a genuine causal chain reconstructing how the makers fell into sleep. This is B's strongest structural move: the backstory IS a sequence, and the fragments mirror it.
- The arc carries forward-looking `next_beat_trigger` and `next_beat_stakes` (Devrant's crack surfacing in the foam fields; Soren opening the host-vault before the cold-glyph is read). This is sharper *next-scene* authoring than A.
- Lovely concrete mechanics: `the-cold-glyph` ("the held condition that keeps a sleeper under; misread, it is also the precise gesture that wakes one"), `the-host-vault-key` (wakes maker-vs-carrier depending on reading the glyph first). The "biological-memetic, spreads through meaning then marrow" framing is crisp.
- Soren (quietly preparing to open the host-vault) is a live ticking bomb wired into the arc's next beat — strong dramatic seed.
- **Weaknesses vs A**: (1) Every single edge is timestamped `800` (the present) except the maker-history events — so the present-tense layer is *temporally flat*; there is no dated rising-action clock among the colonists the way A has (fever/vote/stir on days 40-110). (2) Thinner present cast: 4 colonists, no faction schism (no Ashline equivalent), only one implied belief-conflict (Soren vs Mara) and **no explicit `belief` nodes at all** — A's three-way ideological fight is absent. (3) `the-quarantine-sleep` is dated day800 and the arc edges are also 800, conflating deep history with "now."

### A vs B

| Axis | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | 9 — dated present clock + causal fever/crack chain | 8 — clean maker-history chain, but present layer temporally flat (all day-800) |
| Causal/Structural depth | 9 — two causal layers (maker abandonment + dated present clock) | 9 — explicit `caused_by` maker-history chain + next-beat authoring |
| Thematic richness | 9 — `curse/the-reading-spreads-it`, 3-way belief war, Ashline schism | 7 — strong premise, but no belief nodes, no faction schism |
| Novelty/Surprise | 7 — memetic-lore vector is the fresh idea | 7 — cold-glyph wake/keep duality, maker-sleep pivot |
| Dramatic potential | 9 — best-stated arc (win/fail/clock) + schism + child-stakes | 8 — strong next-beat triggers but fewer competing forces |
| **Total** | **43** | **39** |

**WINNER: World A.** Both share the skeleton and both wire the prerequisite chain correctly, but A populates the *present* with a dated, causally-linked clock (blind crack → fever → stir → vote → keystone) AND a three-way ideological war with a faction schism, while B's present layer is temporally flat (everything stamped day-800) and carries no belief nodes or competing factions. B's dated maker-history is elegant, but A has both that backstory logic AND a richer live stage.

**Filler/scaffolding flag:** Neither leaks step-numbering into prose (both keep `step: "0-proof"` etc. as metadata only — clean). Mild procedural signature in BOTH: the 3-fragments-per-artifact drop matrix is formulaic and includes out-of-sequence drops. B's uniform `800` timestamp on every present-tense edge is a faint template/batch signature (a single fill value rather than authored times).

## Theme 3 — crowded-trade-federation

Both worlds share the premise: a frontier bazaar where five living merchant-factions (Vossan Syndics, Khariti Drift, Oseni Assayers, Melun Cartel, Rho Collectors) trade "ghostlight-amber," whose mined-origin provenance is a forgery; the real source is a living maker-species. The mystery is a `precedes`-ordered provenance chain peeling lies back to the true origin. A human trader (Mara) gets pulled in.

### World A
- Mystery is a 5-fragment chain. The `order` array reads true-origin → authentic-transfer → smuggled-source → khariti-carried → forged-mined (oldest to newest), with `precedes` edges wiring `true-origin precedes authentic-transfer precedes smuggled precedes khariti precedes forged`. **Coherence catch:** the `mystery.order` is presented oldest-first while the *narrative discovery* runs newest-lie-first (the curio's top seal is what Marlena hits first). The `chain_position` facts ("newest-lie", "third", "second", "first-authentic", "true-origin") make the intended peel-back legible — but the mystery `order` array lists discovery in chronological-true order, not reveal order, which is a slight internal tension between "order of uncovering" and "order of events." Each fragment carries an explicit `claim` vs `truth` pair — a genuinely elegant device for a forgery mystery (the document says X, the truth is Y).
- Each fragment maps cleanly to one faction's artifact (Vossan seal → forged-origin, Khariti manifest → carried-it, Melun lading → smuggled, Oseni true-seal → authentic, first-finder token → true-origin) and each `reveals` the right civ. Tight per-faction provenance laddering.
- Strong dated present-tense event chain with causal links: cheap-sale(10) → buyback-offer(20, caused_by sale) → assayer-bribe(35) → manifest-handoff(50) → runs-ambush(65, caused_by handoff). A real escalating investigation with a violence beat. This is the standout — a playable noir trace.
- Rich, individuated cast wired to artifacts: Marlena (tracer), Dret (wants curio back), Saanu (dissident assayer), Captain Yer (manifest-keeper), Sho Melun ("rather you died than said so"), Rho-archivist (funds the hunt). Belief pair `provenance-is-power` + `the-mined-myth` ("the fiction that lets everyone pretend no living maker is being mined") is the thematic core, wired to holders.
- Best single image: `amber-hums-when-near-kin` — "hums louder the closer it comes to more of itself… the first crack in the mined-myth." A diegetic clue that physically betrays the lie. Flavor lore (`syndic-tariff-control`, `collectors-blind-spot`) genuinely characterizes each faction's role in sustaining the forgery economy.
- Weakness: heavy cross-drop matrix again (each artifact drops 3 fragments, some out of chain-position), diluting clean attribution. A handful of location nodes (`counterfeit-row`, `melun-runs`, `collectors-sanctum`) are atmospheric but lightly connected.

### World B
- Reframes the SAME world as a **dated historical saga** (day100 → day500), where the mystery `precedes` chain is wired into an explicit `caused_by` event spine: vossan-first-amber(100) → oseni-seal-the-lie(200) → rho-buy-the-story(200) → oseni-schism(300) → melun-flood-the-fakes(400) → the-contested-deal(400, climax). This is a genuine multi-generation causal history — B's biggest strength. The lie is *born in a first signature* and compounds across eras.
- Only 4 fragments (vs A's 5), but each is sharper and the `precedes` order is unambiguous and forward-chronological: never-mined → seal-can-be-unmade → relic-honest-paper-forged → drift-knows-source. The `the-seal-can-be-unmade` fragment is the cleverest single idea in either world: "the same master die that certified the forgery is the only instrument that can certify the truth… the Cartel floods the market to make any seal meaningless before the real one is used." That's a real causal lever explaining a faction's whole strategy.
- The climax `the-contested-deal` is excellent: four people each hold one link of the true chain, all four factions reach for it at once. This converges the mystery into a single dramatic transaction — far more focused than A's diffuse "trace continues."
- The present-day cast (day500) is tightly wired: Mara owns the route-log, Dev Haldane (Melun payroll) wants it destroyed, Sable (smuggled the confession, betrayed_by Haldane, threatened by Cartel), Quen (Rho heir's agent, "completing the chain ruins the house that raised him"). Each owns exactly one of the four chain-links. `arcs/the-deal-nobody-dares-close` has a concrete `next_beat_actor`/`next_beat` (Mara must pool or sell alone). Cleaner four-handed standoff than A.
- **Weaknesses / catches:** (1) The day timestamps collapse — three distinct events all share day200, two share day400 — so the "dated saga" is coarser than it looks (era-buckets, not a real timeline). (2) A spurious/garbled edge: `colonists/mara-osei-vance owns factions/humans` (owning a faction is nonsense — almost certainly a slot-fill error; should be `member_of`). (3) `civs/the-rho-collectors sought_by colonists/quen-arden` reads backwards (the agent is sought by/serves the house, not the house seeking the relic via... it's inverted). (4) No `belief` nodes at all — A's `mined-myth` thematic frame ("pretend no living maker is being mined") is absent; B states the living-source reveal only in one fragment. (5) Fewer locations (only two, and `ledger-salt-flats` is explicitly the "never worth lying about" foil — a nice idea but a near-inert node). (6) Cross-drop matrix is as heavy as A's and *more* scrambled (e.g. `rho-clean-chain-relic` dropped at day200 but `drift-knows-source` it drops is a day300 fragment — timestamp inconsistency).

### A vs B

| Axis | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | 8 — claim/truth pairs + chain_position are crisp; order array is chrono not reveal-order | 7 — strong causal spine but `owns factions/humans` is a broken edge + collapsed day-buckets |
| Causal/Structural depth | 8 — dated investigation chain w/ caused_by + ambush | 9 — multi-era `caused_by` saga + four-link convergence climax |
| Thematic richness | 9 — `mined-myth`/`provenance-is-power` beliefs, "no living maker is being mined" | 7 — strong "lie born in first signature," but no belief nodes, living-source under-developed |
| Novelty/Surprise | 8 — `amber-hums-when-near-kin` diegetic tell | 8 — `the-seal-can-be-unmade` (same die certifies truth) is the sharpest idea here |
| Dramatic potential | 8 — noir trace w/ funded hunt + violence | 9 — four-handed standoff, one record sellable once, `deal-nobody-dares-close` |
| **Total** | **41** | **40** |

**WINNER: World A (narrowly).** Both are strong noir-economy worlds. B has the more ambitious causal architecture (a multi-era `caused_by` saga converging on a four-link climax) and its single best idea — the seal that unmakes its own lie — outshines anything in A. But B is dragged down by a genuinely broken edge (`mara owns factions/humans`), collapsed era-bucket timestamps that undercut the "dated saga" claim, an inverted Rho edge, and the absence of any belief layer. A is more thematically complete (the `mined-myth`/living-maker frame, the humming-amber tell, the three-way belief wiring) and internally cleaner. A close call; B's structural reach nearly wins it but its slot-fill errors cost it.

**Filler/scaffolding flag:** Both worlds keep fragment text clean of numbering. The 3-drops-per-artifact matrix is the procedural signature in BOTH and in B it's mildly self-contradicting (drop-edge timestamps don't match the dropped fragment's era). B additionally shows a clear slot-fill scaffolding error: `colonists/mara-osei-vance owns factions/humans` (an edge-template populated with the wrong target).

## Theme 4 — generation-ship-identity

Both worlds share a genuinely strong premise: colonists ("Foundlings"/"Returnees") arrive on a world whose architecture fits their bodies because the vanished makers WERE them — humans who committed an atrocity, then deleted themselves and scattered their bloodlines to return blind. Three maker-factions: the makers proper, an archivist-caste who performed the erasure (Cleaners/Redactors), and a dissenter-caste who hid counter-records (Mourners/Refusers). The mystery is a 6-fragment `reveals` chain ending in "It Was Us." This is the deepest theme in the set, and both worlds execute it at a high level.

### World A
- 6-fragment `reveals` chain (`cradle-fits → blank-panel → two-colonies → choice-made → self-erasure → it-was-us`) with the chain wired backwards-correctly (`blank-panel reveals cradle-fits`, etc.) and `cradle-fits` already `revealed: true` (the colony has the first memory). Strong dramaturgical state: Anwen has 4 of 5, one vault from the truth.
- A names the buried crime CONCRETELY: `figures/the-silenced` — a sibling colony deliberately let starve so this one could live. "extinct-by-omission." This is the single best worldbuilding decision across both worlds in this theme: the atrocity is specific, morally legible, and tragic (a famine ledger, "food for one"). It gives the abstract "forgetting" real moral weight.
- Deep dated maker-history spine with causal links: m1-second-seeding(-3000) → m2-famine-ledger(-2900, caused_by) → m3-the-silence(-2880, caused_by) → m4-vote-to-forget(-2800) → m5-dispersal(-2700). Negative day-stamps cleanly separate deep past from the present (day 40-50). Each maker-figure is individuated and tied to an event AND an artifact: the Quartermaster (made the arithmetic undeniable), the Ferryman (tended the Silenced, "returned and never spoke again"), the Cartographer (burned the colony off the sky, "her confession hidden in the precision of the lie"), the Scatterer, the Last Witness, the First Cleaner ("carved a single flaw into the seals… as if some part of him could not bear to be obeyed completely").
- `artifacts ... records events` edges: the Starved Chart `records` the famine-ledger, the Edited Monument `records` the silence, the Mourners' Will `records` the vote. This ties relics to specific historical moments — a third structural layer beyond reveals+drops.
- `locations/the-sibling-coast` ("the far shoreline the charts pretend is open water… where their unmarked dwellings still stand under the dunes") is a superb diegetic location — a place a colonist could physically stumble on the crime. `cistern-stone-cut` is explicitly flagged "Resources here are scenery, not stakes" — honest, but a near-inert node by its own admission.
- Belief pair `clean-inheritance` vs `owed-truth` is the philosophical core, wired to the two maker-factions AND to the Cartographer. Present cast (Anwen/Rhys/Imo/Jax) each embodies a stance; the arc `the-reckoning` is explicit: "choose to know the crime and carry it, or seal the Archive and forget again — repeating, exactly, the choice that damned them."

### World B
- Same 6-fragment chain (`hands-that-fit → vote-to-vanish → clean-silence → buried-copy → weight-of-the-secret → it-was-us`), `hands-that-fit` revealed:true, same correct backwards `reveals` wiring. Structurally a near-twin of A.
- Maker-history spine is even more philosophically pointed: `the-question-raised`(100, "whether a people may delete themselves from their children") → `vote-carried`(200) → `great-redaction`(300) → `buried-argument`(400) → `the-scattering`(600, climactic). The framing "Everything afterward is the working-out of a vote that should have gone the other way" is excellent. The First Doubter's question as "the oldest fingerprint on the whole design, the seed every recovered memory grows back toward" is a beautiful structural metaphor.
- B's maker-figures are sharper as MORAL portraits than A's: the Last Editor ("told himself it was an accident; his own relics record that he chose it"), the Keeper of the Copy ("decided the descendants deserved the damnation more than the comfort"). The Refusers' psychic_signature "a voice that kept a copy because someone had to" and "the ache of writing a letter you will never see opened" is the best prose in either world.
- The present-day cast has the strongest single character beat in the theme: Iris Sandoval, the engineer whose seal "will open for her because, in a sense it terrifies her to admit, she is the one who cut it." That collapses the deep-past and present into one body — a stronger identity-horror payoff than A's Jax.
- **The decisive weakness vs A:** B *refuses to name the crime*. `lore/the-weight-of-the-secret` reads: "the thing buried. **(Per this world: a cure withheld because it could never be shared fairly...)**" — the parenthetical "(Per this world: ...)" is **scaffolding/authoring-instruction leakage into the fragment prose itself** — a template slot describing what *should* go here rather than committing to it. A names a specific, devastating atrocity (a starved sibling colony); B gestures at a generic placeholder ("a cure withheld") wrapped in a visible authoring directive. This is the difference between a finished artifact and a templated one.
- Minor: B's locations (echo-resin-seam, vault-salt-flats) are both explicitly "incidental"/"never the point" — honest but inert, and B has no equivalent to A's Sibling Coast (a place where the crime is physically present). B's drop-edge timestamps are all uniform `700`, a faint batch signature; A's are absent on drops (cleaner). B has no `records` edge layer tying relics to specific events the way A does.

### A vs B

| Axis | A | B |
|---|---|---|
| Coherence | 9 — reveals chain + dated history + `records` layer all consistent | 8 — equally clean chain, but a fragment carries an unresolved "(Per this world: ...)" placeholder |
| Causal/Structural depth | 9 — three layers: reveals + caused_by history + artifact `records` events | 8 — reveals + caused_by history, no `records` layer |
| Thematic richness | 10 — the Silenced (starved sibling colony) gives the forgetting a concrete, tragic crime | 9 — superb on the *ethics* of erasure but the crime itself is an unfilled placeholder |
| Novelty/Surprise | 9 — Sibling Coast under the dunes; Cartographer's "confession in the precision of the lie" | 9 — Iris "she is the one who cut it"; "the working-out of a vote that should have gone the other way" |
| Dramatic potential | 9 — reckoning arc + Jax stalling at the vault + a findable crime-site | 9 — vault-standoff, Iris's identity-horror, equally strong arc |
| **Total** | **46** | **43** |

**WINNER: World A.** This is the strongest pair in the set and the closest on prose quality — B arguably has the finer sentences and the single best character (Iris). But A wins on the thing the theme most needs: it COMMITS to a concrete, morally specific buried crime (the deliberately-starved sibling colony, the Silenced), and wires it through a dated causal history, a `records` relic-layer, and a findable crime-site (the Sibling Coast). B leaves the crime as an explicit authoring placeholder ("(Per this world: a cure withheld...)") in the fragment text — scaffolding leakage that an unfinished artifact would show and a finished one would not.

**Filler/scaffolding flag:** World B leaks an authoring directive into fragment prose: `lore/the-weight-of-the-secret` contains "**(Per this world: a cure withheld because it could never be shared fairly...)**" — a template instruction left visible in the shipped artifact. Both worlds carry the formulaic 3-drops-per-artifact matrix and self-flag their resource locations as "scenery"/"incidental" (honest but near-inert nodes). B's uniform `700` drop-edge timestamp is a mild batch-fill signature.
