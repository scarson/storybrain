**World B**

## THEME 3: extinction-plague-vector

### Graph analysis

**World A**
Mystery chain: proof → identify → trigger → keystone → assemble. Order_relation = "prerequisite" — a strict procedural protocol, not an epistemological revelation chain. Five lore nodes. The central mechanic: the vector "sleeps in the relics' lore and the carriers' marrow, stirring whenever a seal is broken" — so reading relics out of order causes the outbreak. This procedural danger makes the mystery chain a live risk structure, not just backstory. Three civs: cradlekeepers (makers, dormant, possibly safe), stillborn-host (dormant carriers, indistinguishable from makers), vector (the agent). The "you cannot tell which vault holds which" is the core horror. Three present colonists: Mara (strict order), Devrant (already cracked one out of order), the-translator (exposed through lore-reading). The arc explicitly states: fail-state is "cracking a relic out of order ... extinction by the player's own hand." Drift-carrier-ninth as the live threat with threshold-landing inside its range. However: the historical event sequence in A (wars, schisms, exploitations at days 100–800) is a generic procedural graph without narrative specificity — who were the cradlekeepers? Why did the vector arise? What was the conflict? The artifact descriptions are bare archetypes (cure-fragment, recipe-shard, dormancy-trigger, containment-keystone) with no named details or physical character.

**World B**
Same mystery chain (proof → identify → trigger → keystone → assemble), same order_relation ("prerequisite"), same three civs. But B adds substantial texture: the vector is specifically "a memetic nanite-swarm that propagates through the relics' own lore" — so the-translator's fever isn't just exposure, it's an infected reading. The curse node "the-reading-spreads-it" is a dedicated mechanic tying the lore-reading act to the contagion, which is absent in A. B has six named artifacts with specific hazard levels (proof-relic: low; identification-shard: medium; dormancy-trigger: high; containment-keystone: extreme; cure-fragment-foam: medium; sealed-vault-key: high). The hazard gradient directly incentivizes the correct order — open them wrong and each carries the hazard it warns about. B adds five colonists rather than A's three: sergeant-vo (burn faction), dr-amein (rescue faction), child-tam (stakes embodiment). The ashline-creed as a named splinter faction with a distinct belief ("every-seal-is-a-lie") adds a third political option beyond mara vs. devrant. The event sequence in B has named, specific events with causal edges: planetfall-inside-the-range → first-relic-cracked-blind → translator's-fever → carrier-stirs → ashline-vote, each with day stamps and causal links — a living timeline rather than generic war/schism slots. The still-vault (where the half-seal still hangs around its keystone cradle) and the marrow-reliquary (where the stillborn-host sleeps in dormancy-salt) are named distinct locations tied to specific stakes. B's lore/locate-the-keystone-relic correctly identifies "the still-vault" as the revelation, integrating location into lore revelation. B also has the event "the-keystone-located" at day 110, creating a mid-game clock marker.

## THEME 4: crowded-trade-federation

### Graph analysis

**World A**
Mystery chain: true-origin-living-maker → oseni-authentic-transfer → melun-smuggled-source → khariti-carried-it → forged-mined-origin. Order_relation = "precedes." This is a provenance chain traced deepest to newest: the first fragment is the oldest link (living maker), the last is the newest lie (Syndic seal). The mystery order is structured as an archaeology of forgery. Five present factions (vossans, khariti, oseni, melun, rho-collectors), all living, each owning a node in the provenance chain. Three named human PCs (marlena, idris, dret as antagonist, captain-yer, sho-melun). The humming-curio is at the center and drops fragments forged-mined-origin, true-origin, and khariti-carried-it across three chain positions — somewhat over-generous drops. The historical event sequence is sparse (generic discoveries, exploitations, a war, and a schism) with minimal integration. The arc (provenance-deal) names the Rho-Collectors as funders and Melun as physical threat. Flavor lore (amber-hums-near-kin, syndic-tariff-control, khariti-neutral-memory) provides world color. One problem: the mystery order runs true-origin FIRST (oldest link) to forged-origin LAST (newest lie) — but for a tracer starting with the curio in hand, they encounter the forged-mined-origin (the topmost lie) first. The mystery order is counter-intuitive for a player working forward in time; a player would naturally peel the forgery layers newest-to-oldest, which reverses the stated chain order. This is not a logical error in the world, but it creates a structural mismatch between how discovery unfolds and how the chain orders revelations.

**World B**
Same five-faction setup, same ghostlight-amber commodity, same provenance peel concept. B's mystery order: true-origin-living-maker → oseni-authentic-transfer → melun-smuggled-source → khariti-carried-it → forged-mined-origin — identical order. But B fixes the discovery-vs-order problem by having named events sequence the actual investigation: cheap-sale (day 10) → buyback-offer (day 20) → assayer-bribe (day 35) → manifest-handoff (day 50) → runs-ambush (day 65). This event spine creates the experience of the provenance peel as lived drama rather than abstract chain. Each event causes the next and involves specific artifacts. B's artifact set is more precise: the-vossan-seal-of-origin (topmost lie), the-drift-manifest, the-melun-bill-of-lading, the-oseni-true-seal, the-first-finders-token — each carries a single primary fragment with clear physical identity. A's artifacts are less specifically named (forged-provenance-scroll-26, broker-token-4, etc.). B adds old-saanu (dissident assayer) as a named figure with a role in the chain (reads the true-seal for a price), and the-rho-archivist as a more specifically characterized funder. B adds the-collectors-sanctum as a location (sealed vault of relics with stories no one has verified) — a nice thematic mirror for the broader "provenance is fiction" theme. B also adds collectors-blind-spot flavor ("the Rho-Collectors pay for clean stories and never check them") — a sharper irony. B lacks an arc node but the event chain serves the same structural function. One issue: B's mystery.order lists the fragments in the reverse of what the stated chain order implies — mystery.order starts with forged-mined-origin in the list but then B's edge "lore/true-origin-living-maker precedes lore/oseni-authentic-transfer" and mystery.order itself has true-origin FIRST in the order field. Let me recheck: B's mystery.order = [true-origin, oseni, melun, khariti, forged]. The precedes edges: oseni precedes melun, melun precedes khariti, khariti precedes forged, true-origin precedes oseni. So the order IS true-origin first. But B's fragments list = [forged, khariti, melun, oseni, true-origin] (newest-to-oldest). The ORDER field contradicts the FRAGMENTS list order — this is a fragment vs. order list mismatch in B. A also has fragments = [true-origin, oseni, melun, khariti, forged] matching order field. So A has no mismatch; B has a mismatch between the fragments array order and the order array (fragments lists newest-first, order lists oldest-first). This is a minor structural inconsistency in B's schema.

## THEME 5: uplift-nursery-world

### Graph analysis

**World A**
Mystery chain: method-1-seeding → method-2-precedent → method-3-attention → method-4-shaping → method-5-harvest-ahead. Order_relation = "reveals." The five steps are logically ordered: world was designed (1), there was a prior crop (2), the tools are tests not gifts (3), we're being actively edited (4), harvest is ahead (5). The flavor lore nodes (flavor-0 through flavor-18) are numerous but repetitive: several say the same thing ("an attention-bell rings when greed crosses tending") verbatim. Duplicate lore text detected at flavor-6 and flavor-10 (identical), flavor-7 and flavor-11 (identical), flavor-2 and flavor-4 (identical), flavor-0 and flavor-12 (identical). This is filler padding — the same sentences repeated under different slugs rather than distinct world-building. The artifact set includes multiple instances of each archetype attributed to different civs (loam-tenders-gene-loom-4, gardeners-gene-loom-32) with no differentiation in what they specifically reveal. Four "seers" (seer-16, seer-21, seer-32, seer-33) all share identical roles ("sensing-one, feels the gardeners' attention") with no individual character. No named active human colonists with specific stances. The Gardeners, Loam-Tenders, and First-Cultivars are well-differentiated as a three-tier stewardship hierarchy. The "too-green-valley" and the arc "are-we-the-harvest" are strong. But the world lacks named humans who can drive the story.

**World B**
Identical mystery chain and order. But B adds substantial texture: four named colonists with specific fears and roles — maren (sensing-one, "first to call the valley too aimed out loud"), osei (loom-warden who "measured the loom's output against his own children's blood-work and stopped sleeping"), vela (bell-ringer who "argues the alarms are superstition"), idris (shrine-keeper "no longer sure of the difference between gratitude and submission"). These are not generic roles; each colonist embodies a specific angle of the colony's dilemma. B also adds named locations beyond the resource seams: the-soil-record (where the first-cultivars' history is readable), the-shrine-rows, the-loom-hollow, each with distinct functional roles. The two human sub-factions are more precisely named and characterized: the-grateful (Tenders, "you do not bite the hand that waters you") and the-harvesters (Yield Bloc, "if the world was made it can be unmade — take while you can"). The beliefs/we-are-the-crop node adds a third belief structure: the dread itself. The named events (the-first-bell, the-precedent-found) give the world a live timeline. B's flavor lore is varied — no duplicate text. Each artifact drops a unique combination of method-lore and specific notes (note-loom-edits-host: "Osei measured ... and stopped sleeping"; note-key-fits-us: "the inherited key's teeth match a lock found inside the human genome — a lock that was not there at landfall"). This last note is the most memorable detail in the theme: the lock appeared in the genome after landfall, which means the gene-looms are already running. The lineage-key is a unique artifact from the first-cultivars — a key left by the previous crop that fits a lock the Gardeners built into the human line. No equivalent exists in A.

### Notes to the reader

World A dominates the mystery-chain craft for historical/identity themes where multi-agent conflict with named historical figures and specific causal chains is what matters (generation-ship-identity is particularly strong). World B dominates the present-tense stakes themes where named live colonists, differentiated artifacts with hazard gradients, and named event timelines matter more than historical depth. In extinction-plague-vector and uplift-nursery-world especially, B shows richer character and mechanic design. The most structurally weak entry in the sample is World A's uplift-nursery-world, which has literal duplicate lore text and anonymous seers. The strongest single world is World A's generation-ship-identity, which has near-perfect mystery-chain construction.
